# Adams County scrape, group `aurora_brighton`

Pulled 2026-10-31 (UTC) per `scrape_brief_shared.md`. Three sources: Aurora development applications (discovery rank 4), Aurora building permits (rank 6), Brighton Legistar (rank 18). Scripts in `scripts/` (standard library only, shared helper `aurora_brighton_common.py`), raw JSON under `raw/<slug>/` (git-ignored), one CSV per source with the 17 common columns first.

## Assumptions (read first)

1. **County split for Aurora.** Layer 180 (development applications) carries a County field: rows with County = ADAMS are kept on that basis (108 rows). Rows with County null but PropY above 1,695,150 ft (the E Colfax Ave line in Colorado Central state plane, as step 2 derived) are also kept and tagged `county_basis` = "County null, PropY north of Colfax line" (32 rows, all Aurora Highlands, Windler, High Point, Majestic, Denali and similar DIA-area projects); 29 null-County rows south of the line or without coordinates were left out. Layer 44 (permits) has no County field, so the PropY > 1,695,150 cut is in the where clause; it removes 70,861 of 90,227 rows entered since 2024 (66,127 Arapahoe-side, 4,734 with no coordinates).
2. **Record date** is `InDate` (date the AMANDA folder was entered) on both Aurora layers, `date_type` = entered. `IssueDate` is blank on 54 percent of permit rows (every Online Building Folder intake copy), so it is carried as an extra column only.
3. **2023 carry-in.** Layer 180 has a status, so 2023 cases are carried in when FolderStatus is not Retired, Denied & Retired or Retire File (8 rows; 66 closed 2023 rows left out). Layer 44 has no status, final date, expiry or cancel field, so "still active" cannot be tested there: 2023 rows are carried in when they are non-residential construction permits (Commercial Building Permit, Tenant Finish, or a building-class subtype such as Tenant Improvement, Storage Use Building, Racking, Change in Occupancy), tagged `carried_in_2023` = Y (378 rows); 2,388 other 2023 rows (trade, roofing, revisions, right-of-way) were left out. Step 5 should treat the 2023 permits as "possibly still open", not as confirmed active.
4. **Zoning join.** Aurora's zoning polygons (OpenData layer 20, 2,921 polygons, DISTRICT_ID) were joined by point in polygon in state plane feet. Industrial zoning = I-1 (Business/Tech), I-2 (Industrial) and AD (Airport District, which holds the DIA-area logistics and manufacturing parks: Sun Empire, Denali Logistics, Project Waterfall, Dollar General DC, High Point), as discovery recommended; MU-A (Mixed Use Airport) is treated as commercial-industrial. Overlay districts (APZ, BAFB) are tried last so the base district wins where polygons stack. Zoning is today's zoning, not zoning on the record date.
5. **"Industrial property" on the permit layer** (no land-use field) = industrial zoning or an industrial permit subtype (Factory Use, Storage Use, Hazardous Occupancy, Racking/High Piled, fuel storage, Hazardous Materials). A description keyword alone qualifies only for tier A (valuation 250,000 dollars or more), never for the tier B property rules, because words such as EQUIPMENT and TANK also appear on small jobs at homes and shops.
6. **Valuation** on layer 44 is a text field; values of 0 or 1.00 are placeholders (intake copies, revisions) and are treated as no valuation (2,474 rows). Raw text kept in `valuation_raw`.
7. **Company names.** Neither Aurora layer carries an applicant, owner, tenant or contractor field. Layer 180: `company_name` = FolderName (the project name as first filed, which for minor amendments at old plants is the original occupant, for example Michelin Tire Corporation for the RK Mission Critical site), role `project`; blanked when it reads as a person (none did). Layer 44: `company_name` is extracted from the free-text description only when it is a PROJECT codename or a leading segment with a business suffix (LLC, INC, SUPPLY, LOGISTICS, INDUSTRIES and the like); 564 of 9,783 rows carry one. Brighton: the first business-suffixed phrase in the matter title. Nothing without a business term is ever written.
8. **Personal names.** CaseMan (layer 180) and FolderCondition (layer 44, staff notes with names and phone numbers) were not requested. Phone numbers and emails are stripped from permit descriptions, and typed-in first names after ATTN, SPOKE W, PER PE, CALL or CONTACT are replaced with "[name withheld]" (26 rows). Brighton titles that appoint, recognise, honour or proclaim are cut at that keyword and end "[name withheld]" (145 rows); MatterRequester, MatterNotes and MatterText2 (a staff email) are not carried.
9. **Residential rows left out** of the permit CSV: 12,245 (subtype or folder type residential 11,529: Roofing-RT2 4,837, Single-Family Detached 3,435, Solar PV Residential 1,842, Residential Miscellaneous 640, Basement Finish 314, Multi-Family Building 85 and smaller classes; plus 716 trade or revision rows whose description says residential, single-family, basement, duplex, townhome, ADU or apartment). Not left out but flagged N: right-of-way (Public Improvement Permit) rows, signs, commercial roofing, antennae, demolition, fences, temporary offices (`permit_class` = excluded, 2,020 rows).
10. **Dates** are converted from epoch milliseconds in UTC. Layer 180 carries two scheduled intakes dated 2026-11-02; they are kept (`record_date` in the future by two days at pull time).
11. **Brighton** is wholly in Adams except a sliver in Weld; every row is county = Adams, city = Brighton. Brighton's Legistar has only two bodies (City Council, South Brighton General Improvement District). Planning Commission and the Brighton Urban Renewal Authority are not on it (checked `BodyActiveFlag eq 0`, matters and events by body id: nothing), so they could not be pulled.
12. No em dashes anywhere; none were in the sources.

### Aurora development applications (layer 180)
**Source:** City of Aurora "Active Development Applications", Infor AMANDA mirrored to ArcGIS Enterprise. `https://ags.auroragov.org/aurora/rest/services/OpenData/MapServer/180`. Route: the ArcGIS REST `/query` API, one where clause `InDate >= DATE '2023-01-01'` (414 rows, one page), filtered client-side on County and status. Cheaper routes: the hub download is the same layer; no bulk file is needed at this size. Zoning from layer 20 on the same host (6 pages of 500).
**Verdict:** pass. It is the only Aurora source that fired before press in step 2 (Project Waterfall site plan, 53 days ahead), and it names new industrial buildings with square footage.
**Terms and robots:** `ags.auroragov.org/robots.txt` 404 (no file). Open REST endpoint, no key. Aurora Data Download Disclaimer on the hub items: liability and indemnity only, no restriction on automated access or commercial use (recorded at discovery). Requests paced at one a second, user agent `step4-research-pull/1.0`; no 403 was hit with the date-only clause.
**Rows:** 140 written. Source's own count for `InDate >= 2023-01-01`, all counties: 414 (fetched 414). Of those: 179 Arapahoe or Douglas left out, 29 County-null rows south of the line or with no coordinates left out, 66 closed 2023 cases left out, 8 open 2023 cases carried in. 132 rows are dated 2024-01-01 onward (step 2 counted 101 County = ADAMS since 2024 plus 42 null; this pull keeps 101 + 31 of the null rows). County basis: County field 108, PropY line 32.
**Date range:** 2023-03-27 to 2026-11-02 (two scheduled intakes). Since 2024: 1 to 8 rows a month, 2025-05 has none (no Adams-side intake that month in the source), 2025-08 and 2025-09 one each.
**Flagged:** 140 Y, all `all planning and development cases`. `industrial_use` = Y on 61 (use word or square footage says industrial, warehouse, distribution, flex, data center or brewery on 17; the rest by I-1, I-2 or AD zoning or an I-1/I-2 proposed or existing zone). Named new industrial projects in the set: Project Waterfall site plan and plat (755,000 sq ft, 2024-05-24) and its amendment (2026-05-11), Project Frontier at Majestic Commercenter (130,500 sq ft warehouse), Trident Data Center at Windler (860,000 sq ft), High Point Building 11 (276,481 sq ft), High Point 4A and 4B (418,300 sq ft), ABC Cross Docking (33,000 sq ft), Sable Garage Suites flex (109,328 sq ft), Fine Point Business Park (356,000 sq ft, 2023 carry-in), Blue Eagle Logistics Park and Sun Empire East master plans, Aerotropolis Logistics Center amendment, R+L expansion, Michelin loading ramp, Medline guard house, Eagle USA Freight generator pad.
**Null rates:** company_name 0.7% (1 row with no FolderName), site_address 62.1% (87 rows; new-build site plans carry no address until platted), record_date 0%. Also: parcel 62.1%, zip 25.7%, zoning_district 18.6% (26 rows with no coordinates inside a zoning polygon), square_feet present on 20 rows, latitude/longitude 0% (from Shape or from PropX/PropY).
**Duplicates:** 0 on record_id (DANum, or RSN-<FolderRSN> for the 19 "SP AM" minor amendments that carry a legacy case number).
**Fields:** folder_name, da_number, folder_rsn, status, case_type (the layer's yes/no case flags collapsed: site plan new, site plan amendment, plat new, replat, rezoning, conditional use and so on, or "site plan minor amendment" for SP AM rows), use (derived keyword class), square_feet (largest figure in the description), industrial_use, site_acreage, exist_zone, prop_zone, prop_num_dwell, prop_num_hotel, parcel, county_field, county_basis, zoning_district, zoning_name, zoning_class, carried_in_2023, latitude, longitude, created_date, last_edited_date, parser_version.
**Join key:** FolderName (project or codename; the same name is reused across the permit layer's descriptions, for example PROJECT WATERFALL, PROJECT FRONTIER, SUN EMPIRE) and latitude/longitude; Address and Parcel only on 38 percent. FolderRSN links to the AMANDA folder (and to the citizen portal, manual lookup only).
**Refresh:** `python3 scripts/pull_aurora_development_applications.py` (about 10 seconds from cache, 15 seconds live; `--fresh` to refetch). The layer is a daily snapshot that keeps Retired history, so weekly is enough and the whole range can be re-pulled each time (status changes on old rows). `--since` is accepted but the pull always re-reads 2023 onward because the carry-in rule needs status.
**Gotchas:** (1) Not an active-only layer despite its name. (2) About a fifth of rows have no address or parcel; join by name or point. (3) FolderName is the name on the parent site plan, so amendments at old plants carry the original occupant, not the current one. (4) The County field is null on 19 percent of rows; the PropY line fills most of that gap. (5) Cloudflare returned 403 in step 2 for County clauses; this script never sends one.
**Not pulled:** CaseMan (staff name). Arapahoe and Douglas rows. Closed 2023 cases. The portal's application documents and review comments (browser only).

### Aurora building permits (layer 44)
**Source:** City of Aurora "Building Permits" (full layer, 2021 to present), AMANDA mirrored to ArcGIS Enterprise. `https://ags.auroragov.org/aurora/rest/services/OpenData/MapServer/44`. Route: the ArcGIS REST `/query` API, one request per calendar month with `InDate` bounds and `PropY > 1695150` in the where clause, plus an open-ended clause for the current month (47 requests, none paged beyond 2,000 rows; the busiest month has 599). The 1-month and 6-month views (layers 157 and 156) were not used; the full layer carries the same fields and the Address field works there (FULL_ADDRESS and PropStreet do not exist on layer 44, as discovery noted). The where shape with PropY returned 200 on every month; a fallback to a date-only clause with a client-side cut is coded in case the WAF refuses it later.
**Verdict:** pass. Confirms site and stage for every Adams project in the step 2 back-test with equipment-level descriptions (crane foundation, dust collection, equipment packages, conveyor systems); fires after press for announced projects but catches unannounced tenant fit-outs.
**Terms and robots:** as above (no robots file, Aurora Data Download Disclaimer, liability only). 47 query requests plus 4 count requests and the zoning layer, one a second.
**Rows:** 9,783 written (9,405 entered 2024-01-01 onward, 378 carried in from 2023). Source's own counts: 19,366 Adams-side rows since 2024 and 5,050 Adams-side rows in 2023 (24,416 fetched, matches). Left out: 12,245 residential (see assumption 9), 2,388 non-construction 2023 rows. No duplicate OBJECTIDs across pages.
**Date range:** 2023-01-03 to 2026-10-29 (entered). Every month present: 2023 carry-ins 22 to 45 a month; 2024 onward 192 to 352 a month (about 275), no gaps, October 2026 (254) is a full month at pull time. No future dates.
**Flagged:** 3,344 Y of 9,783 (3,118 of the 2024-onward rows). Tier A 685: `valuation 250k+ and industrial zoning` 664, `valuation 250k+ and industrial subtype` 7, `valuation 250k+ and industrial word` 14 (zoning named first when more than one holds; by subtype the 685 are Tenant Improvement 227, Storage Use Building 134, Commercial Miscellaneous 63, Racking 45, Factory Use Building 39, Plan Revisions 32, Electrical 28, Business Use Building 19, Mercantile 17, Mechanical 16; by zoning I-1 331, AD 301, I-2 32, other 21). Tier B3a `valuation 50k to 250k on industrial or commercial-industrial property` 494. Tier B3b `under 50k or no valuation on industrial property` 2,165 (mostly fire alarm and sprinkler head counts, plan revisions and trade permits inside I-1 and AD parks; it is the wide net and will need the `scope_key` and address grouping in step 5). Excluded classes never flag (0 of 2,020). Top flagged addresses: 25315 E 48th Ave (Project Waterfall, 212 rows), 6300 N Powhaton Rd (Project Chorro, 95), 25000 E 56th Ave (Sun Empire 1B, 75), 6611 N Powhaton Rd, 17900 E 32nd Ave, 19080 E 38th Ave (Gateway Park 27), 22100 E 26th Ave, 20321 E 35th Dr (Majestic), 26100 E 68th Ave, 3250 N Himalaya Rd.
**Null rates:** company_name 94.2% (the layer has no name field; 564 rows carry a codename or a suffixed business name from the description: PROJECT WATERFALL 246, PROJECT FRONTIER 56, FOOD BANK OF THE ROCKIES 41, RK INDUSTRIES 29, PROJECT CHORRO 11, PEAK SUPPLY CHAIN 9, ZAYO GROUP 8, CHADWELL SUPPLY 6 and about 85 others), site_address 0.05% (5 right-of-way permits), record_date 0%. Also: valuation blank or placeholder 25.3%, issue_date blank 54.1%, zip 100% (not on the layer), zoning_district 0.02% (2 rows outside every polygon), latitude/longitude 0%.
**Duplicates:** 0 on record_id (Permit_). Each scope appears as an Online Building Folder intake and again as the Limited or Commercial permit days later: 1,605 `scope_key` groups (address plus the first 80 characters of the normalised description) hold 2 or more rows, 3,365 rows in all. Both copies are kept so counts match the source; step 5 should count one per `scope_key` or per address and month.
**Fields:** permit_number, folder_rsn, folder_type (CM, LT, OB, CT, PI, TF), permit_type (FolderDesc), permit_subtype (SubDesc), valuation (numeric), valuation_raw, issue_date, permit_class (building, trade or other, excluded), industrial_word (the keyword that matched), industrial_subtype, zoning_district, zoning_name, zoning_class, tier, carried_in_2023, scope_key, property_rsn, prop_y, latitude, longitude, parser_version.
**Join key:** site_address (Aurora format "25315 E 48TH AVE", no suite; exact match worked on every project in step 2), latitude/longitude, property_rsn (AMANDA property key, shared with layer 180's PropertyRSN), and the project codenames in the description. No applicant, owner or contractor.
**Refresh:** `python3 scripts/pull_aurora_building_permits.py --since 2026-10-01` refetches the months from that date on (one request a month) and rewrites the CSV from the cache (about 1 minute); plain run fetches only months not yet cached; `--fresh` refetches everything (about 2 minutes). The layer updates daily; weekly is enough. Re-pull the last two months each time because valuation is often filled in after intake.
**Gotchas:** (1) No status, final, expiry or cancel date, so cancelled and expired permits look like live ones; the 2023 carry-ins in particular are unverified. (2) Valuation is a placeholder (1.00) on every intake copy and revision, so the Online Building Folder row of a big project is often tier B3b while its Limited Building Permit twin is tier A. (3) AD zoning is treated as industrial; it also covers Gaylord Rockies, the DIA-area hotels and fuel stations, which flag when they clear 250,000 dollars (hotel and mercantile buildings are 36 of the 685 tier A rows). (4) Trade permits (electrical, mechanical, plumbing, fire) at industrial addresses dominate tier B3b. (5) Descriptions sometimes carry staff first names and phone numbers; the known patterns are scrubbed but a name in an unexpected form could remain. (6) The EQUIPMENT keyword is broad; it is only used at 250,000 dollars and up.
**Not pulled:** Arapahoe-side and no-coordinate rows, residential rows, non-construction 2023 rows, FolderCondition. Inspection results, contractor and applicant names are in AMANDA only.

### Brighton Legistar meeting items
**Source:** City of Brighton Legistar (Granicus), Web API client `brightonco`. `https://webapi.legistar.com/v1/brightonco/Matters`, `/Events`, `/Events/{id}/EventItems`; record links go to `https://brightonco.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx` and `MeetingDetail.aspx`. Route: the documented read-only OData API (no key), `$filter` on MatterIntroDate and EventDate, `$top` 1000 paging. One request per meeting for its agenda items (124). Attachments were not opened.
**Verdict:** pass as a small keyword watcher: it named Amprius a week before the state release in step 2, and it carries the Bromley Park PUD amendments, rezonings, annexations and the 2025 incentive policy. Low volume of relevant items (74 of 877).
**Terms and robots:** `webapi.legistar.com/robots.txt` and `brightonco.legistar.com/robots.txt` both 404. The Legistar Web API is published for public read access; discovery recorded "none stated" for terms. 126 API requests at one a second.
**Rows:** 877 written: 873 matters (all matters introduced 2024-01-01 onward; a second page returned 0, so this is the full set) plus 4 agenda items with no linked matter that are not boilerplate (1,696 unlinked agenda items left out as section headers and procedural lines: roll call, approval of agenda, "By the City Manager", consolidated items, swearing-in). 124 meetings since 2024-01-01 (120 City Council, 4 South Brighton GID).
**Date range:** 2024-01-02 to 2026-10-20 (introduced; the 4 agenda-item rows use the meeting date). 25 to 60 rows a month, no gaps.
**Flagged:** 74 Y of 877: annexation 24, plat 16, planned development 10, agreement 10, rezoning 8, incentive 5, use by special review 1. Titles that match a rule but read as residential only (apartments, housing, R-2/R-3 rezonings, subdivision filings with no non-residential word) are N with `residential` = Y (25 rows). Proclamations and minutes are always N. Items worth a look: Bromley Park PUD 32nd to 39th amendments (the industrial area), Grein Property PD and annexation (88.3 acres), Telluride Business Park development agreement amendment (ID-183-26), the Economic Incentive Policy (ID-41-25), Case Farms PUD rezoning, the Platte Land and Water purchase and sale. The incentive rule catches ID-72-24 (Amprius Technologies incentive amendment, 2024-02-21, role grantee), ID-203-24 (Olive Garden Holdings incentive amendment) and an executive session on economic development incentives (ID-115-24, 2024-03-27, no company named: that is the earliest possible tell of a negotiation). The data-center moratorium ordinance ID-300-26 (2026-10) is N under the rules but worth knowing for any data-center lead in Brighton.
**Null rates:** company_name 92.1% (69 rows carry a business-suffixed name from the title; 57 of those are contractors on contract awards, role `contractor`, 2 are incentive grantees), site_address 100% (not on the source), record_date 0%. Also: agenda_date 0%, event_dates 0.1% (one matter never reached an agenda), enactment_number present on resolutions and ordinances.
**Duplicates:** 0 on record_id (MatterFile, or EI-<EventItemId>).
**Fields:** matter_id, body, matter_status, intro_date, agenda_date, passed_date, enactment_number, event_dates (every meeting the matter appeared at), last_action, last_action_date, event_id, residential, parser_version.
**Join key:** project and PUD names in the title (Bromley Park, Brighton Crossing, Grein Property, Telluride Business Park) to the Brighton Planning Development Status layer (rank 9) and to the Brighton permit PDFs; company name on incentive and contract items. No address.
**Refresh:** `python3 scripts/pull_brighton_legistar_items.py --since 2026-10-01` (refetches matters and the meetings from that date, about 20 seconds); plain run reuses the cache; `--fresh` refetches all (about 2.5 minutes). Weekly, after the Tuesday council meetings.
**Gotchas:** (1) Matter status is "Agenda Ready" on 872 of 873 rows; the API's status field is not maintained, use `last_action` and `passed_date` instead. (2) Titles are long boilerplate ("A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF BRIGHTON, COLORADO, ...") so keyword rules fire on words deep in the title; the 500-character description keeps the whole title for all but a few. (3) The company extractor stops at connector words, so a name like "BRANNAN SAND AND GRAVEL" is kept whole only because the AND rule was relaxed; unusual names can still be cut short. (4) Planning Commission and BURA business reaches this feed only when it comes to council. (5) "substringof" filters on the API are case sensitive; this script filters by date only and matches keywords locally.
**Not pulled:** attachments and packets, matters introduced before 2024, MatterRequester and staff email fields, meeting video indexes.

## QA done

Every CSV starts with the exact 17 columns; `flag` is only Y or N; no description over 500 characters; no row dated before 2023-01-03; no em dash in any field; row counts match the source counts for the same filters (414 development rows fetched for 414 counted; 24,416 permit rows for 19,366 + 5,050 counted; 873 matters with an empty second page). 60 distinct permit company names and all 140 development folder names were read by eye for personal names (none); 69 Brighton company names all carry a business suffix. The permit `--since` path was exercised (one month refetched, same 9,783 rows).
