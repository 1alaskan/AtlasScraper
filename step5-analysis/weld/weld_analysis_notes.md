## Assumptions (read first)

1. **Scope.** Weld County sites. A row is in scope when its county is stated by the source (water permits), its AIRS ID carries the 123 prefix (air permits; 14 rows whose county column says Weld but whose prefix is not 123 were left out as office addresses), its zip is wholly inside Weld County (Census 2020 ZCTA file, `weld_zip_share.json`, 26 zips wholly inside, 17 straddling), the Census geocoder places the address in Weld County (174 addresses looked up: 65 Weld, 44 Larimer, 17 Boulder, 9 Adams, 3 Broomfield, 36 no match), or the row comes from the Weld permit or planning layers (Frederick, Greeley, Fort Lupton and unincorporated Weld lie wholly in Weld). Rows with a Weld mailing city and no other basis are kept in a separate "county unconfirmed" section (23 rows). Rows in a split city (Windsor, Johnstown, Erie, Longmont, Brighton, Berthoud, Lochbuie, Thornton, Northglenn) that the geocoder places outside Weld were dropped (13 Mile High CRE rows), not kept as unconfirmed.
2. **The points table, word lists, normalisers and fit rules are Denver's, unchanged** (`v3_config.py`). The Denver rebuild was checked byte for byte at the start of this run (2026-11-05, identical). Nothing was tuned to the Weld ground truth.
3. **County layers.** Six of the nine Weld step 3 files are read through the county-layer loader: Frederick building permits as the permit layer (no valuation in the layer, so tier A is a construction permit type with an industrial word, as for Adams County), and the Weld planning cases, Weld site plan reviews, Frederick development applications, Greeley TRAKiT projects and Fort Lupton projects as the planning layer. The loader's holds apply: low-value permit types (signs, fire, racking, generators, trailers, demolition and the like) are held at B3b whatever step 3 said, commercial-use buildings at B3a, and cancelled permits are cut (2 tier A, 18 tier B). Because the Frederick layer carries neither valuation nor a use field, a Weld-only config flag (`LOCAL_DESC_USE_HOLD`) also holds tier A permits whose description says retail, mercantile, restaurant, well pad and the like at B3a (4 rows: two retail core-and-shell permits, a mercantile tenant finish, an oil and gas well pad); the flag is off for Adams and Denver and both rebuild byte for byte.
4. **No slot in the method (Matthew's decision, 2026-11-06: side list, no code change).** The county Tax Incentive Agreements, the assessor sales and the personal property accounts are not scored. They are published beside the list and every ranked row that matches one by exact name (confirmed) or exact street address (possible) carries the match in `next_step` (34 rows). The scores therefore stay comparable with Denver and Adams, and the single best Weld source sits beside the ranking rather than inside it.
5. **Web checks.** 60 top-ranked rows from the second build were checked on the web for what the company makes at the site (one or two lookups each; `web_checks_weld.md` lists the source for every one). 42 were confirmed and applied through `WEB_CHECKS` in the config (15 strong, 10 moderate, 17 weak); 17 could not be confirmed in two lookups and keep the pipeline's own fit (`WEB_NOT_CONFIRMED_V3`); one (an unattached address) had nothing to check. Fit points: strong +15, moderate +6, unknown 0, weak -15. The downgrades are the Weld noise profile the air-permit rule cannot see: gravel pits and dirt mines (J-2 Contracting's Sunset pit, Asphalt Specialties' Marcovich pit, SVW Farms, Colorado Sand), rail transload and fuel terminals (USRL Hudson, Cando Rail, Western Proppants), a dairy digester (CEA Greeley), a landfill (Waste Management), medical waste (Stericycle), a bulk fuel plant (Agfinity), a dairy, a battery storage site, a water utility, a plumbing contractor and a fire district.
6. **Web drops.** 5507 Distilling: the UCC collateral text itself places the distillation equipment on Fifth Street, Loveland (Larimer County), and the Weld zip is a mailing address. The config's `WEB_DROPS` hook reaches UCC tier A and Mile High CRE rows only; this is a tier B lien, so the row stays at rank 8 with the finding in `next_step`. The scripts were not changed for Weld.
7. **Mailing-address trap.** 23 rows sit in the "county unconfirmed" section (`county_basis` = Weld mailing address, county unconfirmed). Eclipse Engineering (rank 21, 2540 S Main St, Erie) is in the main section on its zip share but Erie straddles Boulder and Weld and the parcel's county was not confirmed; Legacy Pie (rank 22) has Denver shops and no Windsor production site on the web.
8. **Person-named entities.** None of the top 60 is a private individual's name; `PERSON_NAMED` is empty for Weld. Registered entities that carry a founder's name (Jim's Automotive Machine Shop, C. Simpson Farms) are businesses and stay.
9. **Local noise cuts.** The water-permit airport cut (`WAT_AIRPORT`) removed 3 rows (airport construction and airline facilities). No other Weld-specific cut was added; the Denver cuts removed, among others, 87 water permits flagged on acreage alone (schools, parks, housing, roads, pipelines), 45 transit and public-works sites, 63 rows of the May to July 2024 industrial stormwater renewal wave (kept as identity evidence), 15 fuel stations and 8 emergency-generator-only air permits. The full cut table with counts is in `v3_report_run3.md`.
10. **EPA registry names.** 9 unattached addresses score 40 or more; none matches a facility in `frs_filtered.csv` (12,404 Weld rows, mostly oil and gas) on house number and street, so `weld_frs_names.csv` is empty. Parcel owner names were not used (they need Matthew's sign-off).
11. **No Atlas Copco data; no contact with anyone; no records requests; no browser-only portal opened; no personal names.** OEDIT code names are never decoded; the 8 OEDIT rows are "possible" at most. The step 2 ground-truth corrections are not applied.
12. **Windsor** has no permit layer in this build (manual download source). Its sites appear only through the statewide sources, the county assessor sales and the incentive agreements.

## Method

1. Scope records to Weld County (assumption 1). 2. Cut known noise, counted per cut (`v3_report_run3.md`). 3. Resolve records to companies by normalised name and address, keeping every name variant (225 company accounts; 4 co-debtor merges, 3 same-address leading-word merges; one documented tie, Hirsh Precision, in `KNOWN_TIES`). 4. Attach address-only records (permits, site plans) to companies by address: 38 addresses named from county permit rows (the Frederick layer's parcel business name, carried as the tenant role), 3 from a state permit operator, 32 attached to a company as possible, 3 by the documented tie, 17 with one unflagged name as a possible occupier, 220 left as their own candidates. A contractor is not the account. 5. Pull every row for each candidate from every source, flagged or not (312 support rows not listed on their own). 6. Score tier A and tier B signals together under the fixed table; rank. 7. Name unattached addresses from the EPA registry (none matched). 8. `verify_v3.py`: 0 problems.

## Review

An independent review agent audited the third build against the skill and the raw data (`review_findings.md`). Verdict: fail, one blocker, five should-fix, five notes; every score, county basis, web check and ground-truth mark it recomputed passed. What changed in the fourth build:

1. **Blocker, fixed.** Hirsh Precision (C35) sat at rank 326 in the unconfirmed section while rank 159 was its exact address from the Frederick layer, not joined. Fixed with a documented tie in the config (`KNOWN_TIES`: exact name plus address on the Frederick layer and the county agreement) and a resolver rule that a company tied to the county's own permit layer is county confirmed. Adams and Denver rebuild byte for byte.
2. **Loader tiers, fixed.** Four Frederick permits (two retail core-and-shell, a mercantile tenant finish, a well pad) scored as tier A construction because the Frederick layer carries the use only in its description. Held at B3a by a Weld-only config flag (assumption 3).
3. **"Occupant" role, fixed.** The Frederick scrape wrote `occupant` for the parcel business name, a role the resolver does not read; 129 rows now carry `tenant` (the brief's role for a named occupier), and 38 addresses carry a possible occupier instead of 15.
4. **Side list labels, fixed.** A match on street address alone is now "possible", not "confirmed".
5. **5507 Distilling (rank 9), not fixed.** The web check places its equipment in Loveland, but the only drop hook reaches UCC tier A and press rows and the row is a tier B lien. It stays in the list with the finding in `next_step`; a config-reachable drop for tier B rows is a method change for Matthew to decide.
6. **Unconfirmed-section labels, not fixed.** Three Mile High CRE press addresses the geocoder could not match (Loveland, Boulder, Johnstown mailing cities) and 13 split-city rows carry the scripts' standard "Weld mailing address, county unconfirmed" wording; the wording is Denver's and the rows are in the right section.
